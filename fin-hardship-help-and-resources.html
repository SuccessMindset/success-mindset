Financial Hardship Help & Resources

If you're struggling financially right now, you are not alone. Learn where to start looking for help with food, housing, rent, utilities, unemployment, and other essential needs.

Facing Financial Hardship? Start Here

Financial hardship can happen for many reasons. A job loss, unexpected expense, rising bills, housing costs, or major life change can make it difficult to keep up with everyday needs.

Asking for help is not a sign that you have failed. Finding reliable information and using available resources can be a practical step toward getting through a difficult period.

When you're facing financial hardship, you don't have to solve everything at once. Start by identifying your most immediate need and finding the right place to ask for help.

Call 211 for Local Assistance

One of the simplest places to start in the United States is 211.

211 connects people with local community resources. Depending on your location and circumstances, these resources may include assistance involving food, housing, utilities, transportation, healthcare, and other essential needs.

Start With 211

If you don't know which local organization or program to contact, 211 can be a useful starting point for finding community resources.

Visit 211.org

Find Government Benefits and Assistance

The official U.S. government website, USA.gov, provides information about government programs and benefits that may be available to people experiencing financial hardship.

Depending on your circumstances, assistance may include programs involving food, housing, unemployment, healthcare, utilities, and other needs.

Eligibility requirements vary by program and location. Always review the official requirements before applying.

USA.gov Financial Hardship Resources

Explore official information about government benefits and assistance programs.

Visit USA.gov Financial Hardship Resources

Help With Food

If you're having difficulty paying for groceries, there may be government programs and community resources that can help eligible households.

Depending on your circumstances, you may want to investigate programs such as SNAP, WIC, local food banks, food pantries, and other community assistance programs.

You can also contact 211 and ask about food resources available in your local area.

Help With Housing and Rent

Housing costs can become difficult to manage quickly after a loss of income or an unexpected financial problem.

If you're struggling with rent or concerned about losing your housing, consider looking for assistance before the situation becomes an emergency.

Contact 211 and investigate official government resources for rental assistance and emergency housing resources.

Emergency Rent Assistance

USA.gov provides information about options for people who need help paying rent.

Visit USA.gov Rent Assistance

Help With Utility Bills

If you're struggling to pay electricity, heating, cooling, or other utility bills, contact your utility provider and ask whether payment arrangements or assistance programs are available.

You can also contact 211 and ask about local utility assistance programs.

One federal program that may help eligible households with energy costs is the Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program, commonly known as LIHEAP.

Utility Assistance

Learn more about utility assistance and local resources through 211.

Visit 211 Utility Assistance

If You Have Lost Your Job

Losing a job can create immediate financial pressure. If you are unemployed, check your state's unemployment insurance system to determine whether you may qualify for benefits.

You can also use USA.gov to learn more about unemployment benefits and other government assistance.

Unemployment Benefits

Visit USA.gov Unemployment Benefits

Be Careful of Financial Assistance Scams

People experiencing financial hardship can unfortunately become targets for scams.

Be cautious of anyone promising guaranteed government money, requesting unusual upfront payments, or asking for sensitive personal information through an unexpected message.

Legitimate assistance programs have eligibility requirements. For trustworthy information, start with official government websites or established community organizations.

Learn About Government Assistance Scams

USA.gov provides information about scams involving supposed government financial assistance.

Visit USA.gov Scam Information

Create a Simple Plan

When you're under financial pressure, having a simple plan can make the situation feel more manageable.

  1. Identify your most immediate need.
  2. Contact 211 or an appropriate local organization.
  3. Check official government resources for programs you may qualify for.
  4. Contact companies or service providers before missed payments become a larger problem and ask whether assistance or payment arrangements are available.
  5. Be cautious about scams and verify information through official sources.
  6. Once your immediate needs are addressed, focus on the next step toward greater financial stability.

Financial Hardship Does Not Define Your Future

When money is tight, it can be easy to feel as though your current situation will last forever.

A difficult financial period is a chapter of your life, not necessarily the definition of your life.

Getting assistance with immediate needs and working toward long-term financial stability can happen at the same time.

Focus on what you can control. Find reliable information. Ask for help when you need it. Make one practical decision at a time.

Sometimes the first step toward a better future is simply knowing where to start.
