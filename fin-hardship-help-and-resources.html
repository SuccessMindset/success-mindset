Financial Hardship Help & Resources

If you are struggling financially, you are not alone. Financial hardship can happen after job loss, unexpected expenses, reduced income, housing problems, or other difficult circumstances. The important thing is to focus on the next practical step instead of trying to solve everything at once.

Facing Financial Hardship? Start Here

When money becomes tight, it can be difficult to know where to begin. Bills continue arriving, important expenses compete for limited income, and the stress can make it harder to think clearly.

Start by identifying the most immediate needs: food, housing, utilities, transportation, healthcare, and income. Then look for legitimate assistance programs that may help reduce the pressure while you work toward greater financial stability.

You do not have to solve your entire financial situation today. Focus on the next useful step.

Call 211 for Local Assistance

One of the simplest places to start is 211.

In the United States, 211 connects people with information about local resources and assistance programs. Depending on your location and circumstances, you may be able to find help with food, housing, utilities, transportation, healthcare, employment resources, and other essential needs.

Find Government Benefits and Assistance

Government assistance programs vary depending on your location, income, household circumstances, and other eligibility requirements.

The official U.S. government website provides information about programs and services that may be available to people experiencing financial difficulties.

Help With Food

If you are struggling to afford groceries, food assistance may be available through government programs, food banks, community organizations, and other local resources.

The most important thing is not to wait until the situation becomes an emergency. If food is becoming difficult to afford, begin looking for assistance as early as possible.

211 can help identify local food resources. You can also explore government benefit information through USA.gov.

Help With Housing and Rent

Housing costs can become overwhelming when income suddenly drops. If you are worried about rent, eviction, or losing your housing, look for assistance immediately.

Depending on your circumstances, assistance may include emergency rental programs, housing resources, local organizations, or other forms of support.

Emergency Rental Assistance

USA.gov provides information about housing and emergency rental assistance resources.

Explore Housing Assistance

Help With Utility Bills

Electricity, water, heating, and other utility bills can become difficult to manage during a period of reduced income.

Contact your utility company as soon as possible if you believe you may have trouble paying. Some providers offer payment arrangements or hardship programs.

You can also contact 211 to locate utility assistance programs in your area.

Utility Assistance

Search for local utility assistance resources through 211.

Find Local Utility Assistance

If You Have Lost Your Job

Losing a job can create immediate financial pressure. If you have recently become unemployed, look into unemployment benefits and employment assistance as soon as possible.

Eligibility and application procedures vary by state, so use official government resources whenever possible.

Unemployment Assistance

USA.gov provides information about unemployment benefits and related resources.

Learn About Unemployment Benefits

Be Careful of Financial Assistance Scams

Financial hardship can make people especially vulnerable to scams. Be cautious when someone promises guaranteed government money, immediate debt relief, or financial assistance in exchange for an upfront payment or sensitive personal information.

Be especially careful with unsolicited messages, social media advertisements, websites that imitate government agencies, and requests for payment through unusual methods.

When looking for government assistance, start with official government websites or established community organizations.

Create a Simple Plan

Once you have looked for immediate assistance, create a simple plan for the next few days and weeks.

  1. Identify your most urgent expenses.
  2. Contact organizations that may be able to help.
  3. Find out which bills have flexibility or payment options.
  4. Look for available income or employment opportunities.
  5. Reduce unnecessary spending temporarily.
  6. Write down your next three financial priorities.

A simple written plan can make a difficult situation feel more manageable because it turns a large problem into smaller actions.

Financial Hardship Does Not Define Your Future

Financial difficulty can be painful and stressful, but a difficult financial period does not have to determine the rest of your life.

Your circumstances can change. Income can change. Employment can change. Expenses can change. New opportunities can appear.

The goal is to keep taking practical steps while protecting your ability to move forward.

Your current financial situation is a circumstance you are dealing with — it does not have to become the definition of your future.

Continue Building Your Success Mindset

Financial challenges can test your confidence, patience, and ability to stay focused. Developing a success mindset does not mean pretending that problems do not exist. It means learning to face problems, identify your next step, and continue moving forward.

Small actions repeated consistently can help rebuild confidence and create momentum.
