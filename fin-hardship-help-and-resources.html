Financial Hardship Help & Resources

If you're struggling financially right now, you are not alone. Financial hardship can happen because of job loss, unexpected expenses, housing costs, medical bills, or other major life changes.

Asking for help is not failure. Finding the right resources, protecting your basic needs, and taking your next step are all part of moving forward.

Where Should You Start?

If you don't know where to begin, start with resources that can connect you with assistance in your local community.

Call 211

211 is a nationwide network that helps connect people with local community resources.

Depending on your location and circumstances, 211 may be able to help you find resources involving food, housing, utilities, transportation, healthcare, and other essential needs.

Visit 211.org

Find Government Assistance

The official U.S. government website, USA.gov, provides information about government programs and benefits that may be available to people experiencing financial hardship.

Depending on your circumstances, programs may provide assistance with food, housing, unemployment, healthcare, utilities, and other needs.

Eligibility requirements vary by program and location. Always check the official requirements before applying.

USA.gov Financial Hardship Resources

Use the official government website to explore assistance and benefits that may apply to your situation.

Visit USA.gov Financial Hardship Resources

Help With Food

If you're having trouble affording groceries, there may be government and community resources available.

Depending on your circumstances, you may want to investigate programs such as SNAP, WIC, local food banks, food pantries, and community assistance programs.

You can also contact 211 and ask about food assistance available in your local area.

Help With Housing and Rent

Housing expenses can become difficult to manage quickly after a loss of income or unexpected financial problem.

If you're struggling with rent or concerned about losing your housing, don't wait until the situation becomes an emergency before looking for assistance.

Contact 211 and investigate official government resources for rental assistance and emergency housing resources.

Emergency Rent Assistance

USA.gov provides information about options for people who need help paying rent.

Visit USA.gov Rent Assistance

Help With Utility Bills

If you're struggling to pay electricity, heating, cooling, or other utility bills, contact your utility provider and ask whether payment arrangements or assistance programs are available.

You can also contact 211 and ask about local utility assistance.

One federal program that may help eligible households with energy costs is the Low Income Home Energy Assistance Program, commonly known as LIHEAP.

Utility Assistance

Learn more about utility assistance and resources through 211.

Visit 211 Utility Assistance

If You Have Lost Your Job

Losing a job can create immediate financial pressure. If you are unemployed, check your state's unemployment insurance system to determine whether you may qualify for benefits.

You can also use USA.gov to learn more about unemployment benefits and other government assistance.

Unemployment Benefits

Visit USA.gov Unemployment Benefits

Be Careful of Financial Assistance Scams

Unfortunately, people experiencing financial hardship can become targets for scams.

Be cautious of anyone promising guaranteed government money, requesting unusual upfront payments, or asking for sensitive personal information through an unexpected message.

Legitimate assistance programs have eligibility requirements. For trustworthy information, start with official government websites or established community organizations.

Learn About Government Assistance Scams

Visit USA.gov Scam Information

Financial Hardship Does Not Define Your Future

When money is tight, it can be easy to feel as though your current situation will last forever.

But a difficult financial period is a chapter of your life, not necessarily the definition of your life.

Getting assistance with your immediate needs and working toward long-term financial stability can happen at the same time.

Focus on what you can control.

Find reliable information. Ask for help when you need it. Make one practical decision at a time.

Keep moving forward.

Continue Building Your Success Mindset

Financial stability is only one part of building a stronger future. Continue developing the habits, confidence, discipline, and focus that can help you move forward.
